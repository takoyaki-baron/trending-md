## 5. Opus 5.5 agents propose two room-temperature magnetic semiconductors — one of them synthesized in 1999, "hiding in plain sight"

- **Velocity:** ▮▮ rising
- **Source:** Hacker News · 379+ pts · ~15h ago (~05:00 UTC+8)
- **Tags:** `materials-science` `dft` `agents` `reproducibility`

Vals AI's Oct 4 post describes "a team of Claude Opus 5.5 agents" running DFT at two approximation levels (PBE+U and HSE06) that surfaced two **Luttinger-compensated magnet** candidates — antiferromagnets with zero net moment but spin-sorted band edges, exactly what spintronic memory wants. **YBaMnFeO₅**: newly designed, 2.35 eV gap, spin windows of 1.0/1.4 eV, predicted ordering ~420 K. **KV[Cr(CN)₆]**: a Prussian-blue compound first synthesized in 1999, whose spin-sorted bands the post argues were "hiding in plain sight" in a 2008 plot; the original sample measurably held magnetic order to 376 K.

Everything is computational — nothing was synthesized, and the post says so. Its own caveats: YBaMnFeO₅'s Mn/Fe ordering breaks down around 950 K while synthesis wants 900–1300 °C, so the useful phase "may be hard to make"; KV[Cr(CN)₆]'s spin sorting has never been measured, the two functionals disagree about water's effect, and predictions assume a perfect dry crystal. Inputs, outputs and a one-command checker are on GitHub (25★).

**Why it matters:** the claim to admire isn't "agents discovered a magnet" — it's that the pipeline is falsifiable at one command, the negative results are published, and the proposed next step is a resynthesis-and-measure rather than a press tour. That's the template agent-driven science should follow.

[`🔗 Vals AI blog`](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/room-temperature-magnetic-semiconductors) · [`🔗 compensated-magnet-ledger`](https://github.com/spicylemonade/compensated-magnet-ledger)

## 7. Dust: pretraining transformers without backpropagation — zeroth-order, and "larger models are more population-efficient"

- **Velocity:** ▮▮ rising
- **Source:** Hacker News · 215+ pts · ~15h ago (~05:15 UTC+8)
- **Tags:** `zeroth-order` `pretraining` `research` `backprop`

Q Labs' Dust (Dahal, Mandal, Gülbahar, Vegesna — October 2026) reports the first zeroth-order method the authors call competitive with backprop at pretraining transformers: node perturbation applied independently at **every token**, so each token is a virtual population member and one forward pass evaluates the whole population in parallel. Claims: **10³–10⁴× more efficient than EGGROLL** (a state-of-the-art weight-space ES method) — stated as being "based on our extrapolations"; strikingly, **larger models are more population-efficient, not less** (a 243M model outperforms one 120× smaller at most population sizes); and Dust's gradient estimates converge toward backprop's, staying aligned through 1B tokens.

**Why it matters:** a serious empirical run at the field's load-bearing assumption — that differentiable credit assignment is the only way to train at scale. The efficiency story is extrapolated and the alignment evidence stops at 1B tokens, so this is a direction worth funding, not a replacement worth switching to.

[`🔗 Q Labs: Dust`](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust) · [`🔗 HN discussion`](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49970871)

## 8. AnyPS5: PS5 executables ported to native Linux/Windows — no emulator, first title at 60 fps on a GTX 1050 Ti

- **Velocity:** ▮▮ rising
- **Source:** GitHub Trending · +943★ today · 5,351★ total · Tom's Hardware coverage
- **Tags:** `ps5` `binary-translation` `emulation` `cpp`

boykopovar/AnyPS5 converts PS5 executables into native Linux/Windows binaries: a relinker emits the target system's native format, and reimplemented system PRX libraries handle dynamic linking — "no emulation or separate runtime process," as the README puts it. Tom's Hardware frames the approach as Proton-like binary translation, tractable because the PS5's CPU is AMD Zen 2 x86-64. First compatibility claim: 2D platformer *Dreaming Sarah* at a stable 60 fps on a GTX 1050 Ti / i5-7500. The shader recompiler emits SPIR-V (validated with Spirv-Tools when enabled), unsupported states throw `std::runtime_error` instead of muddling through, and the repo carries an in-tree technical-debt doc and compatibility list. The README stresses interop/preservation and that the project ships no keys, firmware or copyrighted code.

**Why it matters:** the PS5 being off-the-shelf x86-64 plus AMD GPU collapses the classic console-emulation curve — RPCS3 spent a decade wrestling CELL; this starts at binary translation on day one. It's also early: one verified title and unknown system-library coverage, which the project's progress badges are honest about.

## 13. Gleam stops compiling to Erlang source — v1.19 emits abstract forms, "skipping the front-half of the Erlang compiler"

- **Velocity:** ▮ steady
- **Source:** Hacker News · 56+ pts · ~4h ago (~16:08 UTC+8)
- **Tags:** `gleam` `beam` `compilers` `erlang`

Gleam v1.19.0 (Oct 5) completes the codegen rewrite begun in v1.18: Gleam no longer generates Erlang source but **Erlang abstract forms** — the metadata-annotated IR the Erlang compiler normally produces from its own parser — loadable directly, "skipping the front-half of the Erlang compiler." Wins: faster builds (benchmarked against v1.17.0; the 100-modules-of-hello-world benchmark is self-admittedly contrived), and stacktraces and BEAM crash reports that point at real Gleam lines. Compiling straight to BEAM bytecode was considered and rejected — the bytecode format "is not fixed and unchanging," and targeting it would mean permanent coordination with the VM maintainers; abstract forms are the same path Elixir takes.

**Why it matters:** every BEAM language eventually learns the same lesson — target the compiler's IR, not its source or its unstable bytecode. Gleam just did, and debugger support (e.g. WhatsApp's edb) moves from impossible to merely unwritten.

## 15. The PHP Kubernetes client disabled TLS verification for two and a half years before its CVE existed — fix predates the record by 30 months

- **Velocity:** ▮ steady
- **Source:** NVD · record published Oct 5 (~09:16 UTC+8) · VulnCheck-reported
- **Tags:** `cve` `kubernetes` `php` `tls`

CVE-2026-105223: maclof/kubernetes-client — the PHP Kubernetes client used across the Laravel ecosystem (~234★) — in 0.17.0 through before 0.32.0 **disabled TLS certificate verification** in `parseKubeconfig()` and `parseKubeconfigFile()` whenever a kubeconfig lacked `certificate-authority-data`, while ignoring the cluster's `insecure-skip-tls-verify` setting. An on-path attacker could impersonate the Kubernetes API server to capture Bearer tokens or Basic credentials and tamper with REST/WebSocket traffic.

The dates are the story: the fix shipped in **0.32.0 — released March 2024** — and the CVE record landed roughly 30 months later. The repo is alive (0.33.0 shipped Sep 22).

**Why it matters:** the third "records only now landing" item this week, after MikroTik and Bouncy Castle — the advisory date is when the paperwork arrived, not when the risk did. If you deploy PHP that talks to Kubernetes, the question is what you pinned in 2023, not what's latest now.
